Construction and interpretation

Window centre. The displayed h uses the original window centre as (0, 0). This page samples 391 centres from a tenfold symmetric cut-and-project point set, including the origin. Drag anywhere inside W, or enter coordinates and press Enter / Move. The centre snaps to the nearest checked point. Arrow keys move a focused internal view to a nearby point in the chosen direction.

Tenfold sampling. Candidate labels are taken from the same A₄ lattice and two projection maps as the covering. We interchange the roles of the projection planes: labels are selected by a regular decagonal window in the other projection, and their internal projections supply the centre positions. A circular crop preserves complete 36° rotation orbits; scaling the outer radius to 1 gives 391 points. This is a finite quasiperiodic point patch, with tenfold symmetry about (0, 0). Its rotational symmetry describes the sampling positions, not necessarily each resulting finite covering. The square-grid archive retains the five original constructions; E20′ is available on this page.

Coordinates. If h₀ is the original phase in the source data, the phase used in the calculation is h₀ + h. The internal plot uses fixed coordinates y − h₀. W, its deletion regions and both pentagon acceptance windows B₀ and B₁ move by the same h; lattice projections stay fixed. A dashed outline marks the original W. The physical covering is regenerated from the corresponding accepted labels.

Boundary handling. The exported samples stay away from the boundaries of W, B₀, B₁, all five deletion windows and all 101 progress masks. Membership is checked throughout the exported sample, including the surrounding polygons needed at the observation boundary. These checks concern the finite samples shown here.

Construction selection. L5, L6, L8, L11 and E20 are separately verified selections, not generations of an established recursion. Their deletion regions need not be nested. Changing translation preserves R, t and the construction. A selected label is preserved when it remains accepted and visible; otherwise another centre is selected.

Observation region. R is the radius of the circular physical-space sample in polygon edge lengths. Changing R does not change the windows or infinite-system densities. Internal candidates are shown in a fixed square −2.9 ≤ (y − h₀)ᵢ ≤ 2.9. Accepted centres include the extra surrounding polygons needed to fill the observation circle.

Replacement rule for L5–L11 and E20. For 0 ≤ t ≤ 1, St = SC ∩ tW, where C is one of the five original constructions. In the displayed coordinates the active deletion region is h + St; in source coordinates it is h₀ + h + St. The mask scales about the translated window centre. Intermediate states are compatible partial replacements. Each St is a subset of the selected independent deletion window.

Area ratios for L5–L11 and E20. ft = |St| / |W| and ηt = (3 − τ − 3ft) / (1 − ft), with τ = (1 + √5)/2. Joint translation changes neither area ratio. At t = 1, E20 has η ≈ 0.3822460149. Finite centre counts can change with translation.

E20′. The original W is unchanged. Twenty small added windows outside W and twenty extra deletion windows inside W select a certified local rearrangement, in ten rotation-related orientations. Each move removes and adds two decagons, eight pentagons and four hexagons, with no change in overlap-pair count. All overlaps remain the prescribed decagon thin rhombi. The straight triple has overlap-neighbour degrees 1, 2, 1. The windows and the complete local geometry are taken from the exact certificates.

E20′ progress. For 0 ≤ t ≤ ½ the E20 deletion mask is 2tW. For ½ < t ≤ 1, E20 is complete and each certified marker window grows about its own centre by λ = 2t − 1. Selecting a marker activates its entire local move at once. These markers stay inside the certified, mutually separated windows. η remains exactly equal to E20 throughout this second stage; the original deletion-only formula must not be applied to S′ alone. Added and extra removed areas are equal. At full progress the guaranteed fraction of decagons in the introduced triples is approximately 0.01335% in the infinite system; a finite sample can contain none.

E20′ checks. All 391 translations were checked for the new marker windows and all 50 growth levels, with positive finite boundary clearance. Every occurring move was matched to the certified E20 neighbourhood. Representative finite coverings also passed direct coverage and prescribed-overlap checks at R = 60. The surrounding buffer includes moves that can affect the observation boundary. The “Show a straight triple” button restores the original centre and full progress and selects the middle decagon of the demonstrated triple.

Finite-sample diffraction. The scattering points are all distinct polygon vertices with |r| ≤ R, each with weight one. Vertices shared by several polygons are counted once; polygon centres, coloured fills, highlighted outlines and artificial intersections with the circular crop are not scatterers. The same current geometry supplies both the physical view and the diffraction. Construction, R, t and h can all change the finite-sample intensity; changing the selected label does not.

Fourier intensity and display. Following Fig. 1f, vertices are rounded to a 4096 × 4096 real-space grid in a square of side 192 edge lengths. I(q) = |FFT|² / N, where N is the number of distinct vertices in the observation circle; q is in radians per edge. Colours show log(1 + I) for approximately −10 ≤ qₓ, qᵧ ≤ 10. The colour ceiling is the 99.95th percentile of each sample, as in Fig. 1f; use the numerical intensity readout to compare peaks across samples. Narrow peaks are preserved when the image is reduced to the panel size. There is no rotational averaging or forced tenfold symmetrization. Finite-size peak broadening and variations with h are expected; this is a finite-sample illustration of Bragg diffraction, not an infinite-plane diffraction measure.

Checks and interpretation. All 391 positions are checked for finite boundary clearance and independent local replacements through R = 60 plus a surrounding buffer. Thirteen representative positions also have direct coverage and overlap checks at R = 60: the reference covering, all five full constructions and an E20 partial replacement, for 91 states total. These verify coverage of an inscribed 256-sided region, only prescribed thin-rhombus decagon overlaps and no forbidden or positive-area triple overlaps. These are finite numerical diagnostics supported by the existing construction certificates. Diffraction is computed from the actual displayed configuration, with the circular vertex cutoff described above.

The reference covering and replacements follow the lower-bound construction. The additional restriction of at most two overlapping neighbours per decagon, introduced in the separate upper-bound study, is not imposed here.
